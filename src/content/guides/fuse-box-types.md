Your fuse box — properly called a **consumer unit** — is where your home's electrics are controlled and protected. It's usually under the stairs, in a hallway cupboard or near the front door. What type you have says a lot about how old your electrics are and how well protected you are.

## The main types

### 1. Rewireable fuse boards
Porcelain or plastic fuse carriers that pull out, with a length of **fuse wire** inside. Common in homes wired before the 1970s–80s.

- **No RCD protection**, so they won't cut the power fast enough to protect someone touching a live part
- Fuse wire can be replaced with the wrong rating
- A strong sign the rest of the wiring may be old too — try our [rewire check](/do-i-need-a-rewire/)

### 2. Cartridge fuse boards
Similar to the above but with sealed cartridge fuses. Still usually **no RCD protection**.

### 3. Circuit breaker (MCB) boards without an RCD
A row of switches (MCBs) that trip when a circuit is overloaded. Easier to reset than fuses — but if there's **no "test" button**, there's probably no RCD, so no protection against electric shock.

### 4. Split-load consumer units (one or two RCDs)
Very common from the 2000s onwards. One or two **RCDs**, each protecting a group of circuits. Much safer — but one fault can switch off **half the house**, including the fridge-freezer or lights.

### 5. RCBO consumer units
Each circuit has its own **RCBO** (a circuit breaker and RCD in one). A fault only switches off the circuit it's on. This is what we usually recommend for a new installation.

## Metal, not plastic

Since 1 January 2016 the wiring regulations have required new consumer units in homes to be housed in **non-combustible** enclosures — in practice, metal. An existing plastic unit isn't automatically dangerous, but any replacement will be metal.

## Signs it's time to upgrade

- You have rewireable or cartridge fuses
- There's no RCD ("test" button) protection
- An EICR has recommended it
- You're adding circuits — an extension, a kitchen, a garden office or outdoor lighting
- You're planning a rewire or renovation anyway

## What an upgrade involves

1. We **test the existing circuits** first. RCD protection will trip if there are hidden faults, so any problems are found and explained before the new board goes in.
2. The new consumer unit is fitted, usually in **about a day**.
