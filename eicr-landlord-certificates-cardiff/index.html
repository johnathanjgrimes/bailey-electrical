What is an EICR?

An Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR) is a detailed inspection and test of a property’s fixed electrical installation — the wiring, consumer unit, sockets, switches and light fittings. It shows whether the installation is safe to keep using and highlights anything that needs attention.

Landlords in Wales

Under the Renting Homes (Wales) rules, landlords must make sure the electrical installation is tested at least every five years and give the contract-holder a copy of the report within 14 days of the occupation contract starting. We carry out EICRs for landlords and letting agents across Cardiff and can quote for and complete any remedial work so the property passes.

Understanding the codes

  • C1 – Danger present. Immediate action needed.
  • C2 – Potentially dangerous. Urgent remedial work needed.
  • C3 – Improvement recommended. Not a fail, but worth considering.
  • FI – Further investigation needed without delay.

Any C1, C2 or FI means the report is “unsatisfactory”. We’ll explain in plain English what was found and what it will cost to put right.

Homeowners and buyers

For owner-occupied homes, an EICR is generally recommended every ten years, when you buy a property, or before starting a renovation. It’s a good way to find out whether you need a rewire or consumer unit upgrade before planning other work.

Frequently asked questions

How often do landlords in Wales need an EICR?

At least every five years, and the contract-holder must be given a copy within 14 days of the occupation contract starting.

How long does an EICR take?

Typically a few hours for a flat or average-sized house, depending on the number of circuits and access.

What happens if the report is unsatisfactory?

We'll explain what was found and quote for the remedial work. Once it's done, you'll receive confirmation that the issues have been put right.
